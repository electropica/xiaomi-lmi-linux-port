# Linux 7 battery triage and USB guard — 6 November 2026

A one-hour screen-off trial completed one suspend: kernel s2idle ran for approximately 59 minutes. The endpoint was already charging when sampled, so it is not an isolated discharge measurement. Earlier, an idle watch fired without a subsequent recorded suspend; journal evidence shows the USB guard had released its inhibitor at that time. Neither observation establishes the cause of battery drain.

The USB guard skips the VBUS ADC read when the other connection signals rule out a PC connection; it still reads VBUS while holding an inhibitor. Its decision was compared across 144 synthetic input combinations, and the existing guard tests passed. On the handset, the service was active, released its inhibitor on unplug and reacquired it on reconnect. No energy savings or long-term suspend reliability have been measured.

The battery triage service is on-demand, not enabled at boot. It records system-level gauge readings, sleep inhibitors, suspend events, and sampled process CPU activity; it does not measure per-process electrical energy. Short on-device plugged-in tests verified bounded sampling, stop behavior and some caller attribution. The current source additionally checks an elapsed-boottime deadline after resume; that change has been tested while awake, but not over a real suspend. No wake alarm is scheduled. The optional sleep hook is not installed; its firmware counters are diagnostic signals, not watt measurements. A single on-device read succeeded without requesting suspend.

The read-only analyzer handles older captures without process start times conservatively and refuses CPU attribution when PID reuse cannot be ruled out. Sleep `delay` and `block` inhibitors are reported separately. Captures can contain network and session metadata: do not publish raw records. Run `python3 -m unittest discover -v` in the tool directory. No build or firmware flash is required for these userspace tools.
